# Analysis Methods Index

This directory contains guides and templates for each stock analysis method used by the market research system.

## Methods

### [Fundamental Analysis](fundamental.md)
Evaluate a company's intrinsic value through financial statements, valuation ratios, competitive positioning, and growth prospects. Best suited for medium-to-long-term investment horizons.

### [Technical Analysis](technical.md)
Analyze price action, volume, and chart patterns to identify entry and exit points. Uses indicators like moving averages, RSI, and MACD. Applicable across all timeframes.

### [Sentiment Analysis](sentiment.md)
Gauge market mood through news sentiment, social media activity, insider trading signals, and analyst revision trends. Often used as a confirming or contrarian signal alongside other methods.

### [Quantitative / Statistical Methods](quantitative.md)
Apply mathematical models, factor analysis, and statistical techniques to identify mispriced securities or systematic edges. Includes factor models, statistical arbitrage, and mean reversion strategies.

### Macro / Sector Analysis
Assess the broader economic environment, sector rotation dynamics, interest rate trends, and geopolitical factors to identify which sectors and themes are likely to outperform. (Guide to be added as the system matures.)

## Combining Methods

The strongest picks typically combine multiple methods. For example:
- Fundamental analysis identifies an undervalued company.
- Technical analysis pinpoints a favorable entry point.
- Sentiment analysis confirms that negative sentiment is overdone.
- Quantitative screening validates the signal statistically.

When logging a pick, note the primary method but reference supporting analysis from other methods where applicable.
