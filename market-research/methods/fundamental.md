# Fundamental Analysis Guide

## Overview

Fundamental analysis evaluates a company's intrinsic value by examining its financial health, competitive position, and growth prospects. The goal is to find stocks trading below (for longs) or above (for shorts) their fair value.

## Key Metrics

### Valuation Ratios
- **P/E Ratio (Price-to-Earnings)** — Compare to sector median and historical range. A low P/E may signal undervaluation, but check why it's low.
- **P/B Ratio (Price-to-Book)** — Useful for asset-heavy industries (banks, REITs). Less relevant for tech/services.
- **P/S Ratio (Price-to-Sales)** — Helpful for high-growth companies not yet profitable.
- **EV/EBITDA** — Enterprise value relative to earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization. Good cross-company comparison metric.
- **PEG Ratio** — P/E divided by earnings growth rate. PEG < 1 may indicate undervaluation relative to growth.

### Profitability
- **Gross Margin** — Revenue minus cost of goods sold, as a percentage. Higher is better; watch for trends.
- **Operating Margin** — Measures operational efficiency.
- **Net Margin** — Bottom-line profitability.
- **ROE (Return on Equity)** — How effectively the company uses shareholder capital.
- **ROIC (Return on Invested Capital)** — Compares to WACC; ROIC > WACC indicates value creation.

### Growth
- **Revenue Growth (YoY, QoQ)** — Is the top line expanding? Accelerating or decelerating?
- **Earnings Growth** — EPS trajectory matters more than a single quarter.
- **Free Cash Flow Growth** — Cash is harder to manipulate than earnings.

### Balance Sheet Health
- **Debt-to-Equity Ratio** — How leveraged is the company?
- **Current Ratio** — Can it meet short-term obligations? (Current assets / current liabilities)
- **Interest Coverage Ratio** — EBIT / interest expense. Below 2x is a warning sign.
- **Cash Position** — Cash and equivalents relative to burn rate or debt maturities.

### Competitive Moat
- **Network Effects** — Does the product become more valuable as more people use it?
- **Switching Costs** — How painful is it for customers to leave?
- **Cost Advantages** — Scale, proprietary technology, or geographic advantages?
- **Intangible Assets** — Brands, patents, regulatory licenses.
- **Efficient Scale** — Does the market naturally support only a few competitors?

## Analysis Checklist

1. **What does the company do?** Describe the business model in plain language.
2. **Is it growing?** Revenue and earnings trajectory over 3-5 years.
3. **Is it profitable?** Margins, FCF, and return on capital.
4. **Is it cheap?** Valuation ratios vs. peers and historical range.
5. **Is the balance sheet healthy?** Debt levels, cash runway, upcoming maturities.
6. **Does it have a moat?** Sustainable competitive advantages.
7. **What are the risks?** Regulatory, competitive, cyclical, management quality.
8. **What's the catalyst?** Why would the market re-rate this stock?

## Common Pitfalls

- **Value traps** — A stock is cheap for a reason (secular decline, bad management). Always ask why.
- **Ignoring cash flow** — Earnings can be manipulated; free cash flow is harder to fake.
- **Recency bias** — One good quarter does not make a trend.
- **Ignoring macro** — Even great companies suffer in recessions or hostile rate environments.
