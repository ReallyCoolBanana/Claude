# Fundamental Analysis Guide

## Overview

Fundamental analysis estimates a company's intrinsic value by examining its financial health, earnings power, growth prospects, and competitive position. The core question: is the stock trading below what the business is actually worth?

## Key Metrics

### Valuation Ratios
| Metric | What It Tells You | Watch For |
|--------|-------------------|-----------|
| **P/E Ratio** | Price relative to earnings | Compare to sector median; low P/E may signal undervaluation or declining business |
| **Forward P/E** | Price relative to expected earnings | More useful than trailing P/E for growing companies |
| **P/B Ratio** | Price relative to book value | Below 1.0 may indicate undervaluation; common in financials |
| **P/S Ratio** | Price relative to revenue | Useful for unprofitable growth companies |
| **EV/EBITDA** | Enterprise value relative to operating earnings | Accounts for debt; better for cross-company comparison |
| **PEG Ratio** | P/E adjusted for growth rate | Below 1.0 suggests growth is underpriced |

### Growth Metrics
- **Revenue growth** (YoY, QoQ) -- Is the top line expanding?
- **Earnings growth** (EPS trajectory) -- Is the company becoming more profitable?
- **Free cash flow growth** -- Can the company fund its own growth?
- **Gross margin trend** -- Is pricing power improving or deteriorating?

### Balance Sheet Health
- **Debt-to-equity ratio** -- How leveraged is the company?
- **Current ratio** -- Can it cover short-term obligations?
- **Interest coverage** -- Can it service its debt comfortably?
- **Cash position** -- How long is the runway?

### Profitability
- **Return on equity (ROE)** -- How efficiently does it generate returns for shareholders?
- **Return on invested capital (ROIC)** -- Does it create value above its cost of capital?
- **Net margin** -- What percentage of revenue becomes profit?
- **Operating margin** -- Core business profitability before interest and taxes

## Qualitative Factors

### Competitive Moat
- **Network effects** -- Does the product become more valuable as more people use it?
- **Switching costs** -- How hard is it for customers to leave?
- **Cost advantages** -- Can the company produce more cheaply than competitors?
- **Intangible assets** -- Patents, brands, regulatory licenses?
- **Efficient scale** -- Does the market only support a limited number of players?

### Management Quality
- Insider ownership and recent transactions
- Capital allocation track record
- Transparency and communication
- Compensation alignment with shareholders

### Industry Position
- Market share and trajectory
- Competitive dynamics (consolidating vs. fragmenting)
- Regulatory environment
- Secular trends (tailwinds or headwinds)

## DCF Valuation Template

1. Project free cash flows for 5-10 years based on revenue growth and margin assumptions.
2. Estimate a terminal value using a terminal growth rate (typically 2-3%) or exit multiple.
3. Discount back to present value using WACC.
4. Compare intrinsic value per share to current market price.
5. Apply a margin of safety (typically 20-30% discount to intrinsic value).

## Checklist for a Fundamental Pick

- [ ] Valuation is attractive relative to peers and historical range
- [ ] Revenue and earnings are growing or have a clear catalyst for inflection
- [ ] Balance sheet is healthy enough to weather downturns
- [ ] ROIC exceeds cost of capital
- [ ] Identifiable competitive moat
- [ ] Management is competent and aligned with shareholders
- [ ] Clear thesis for why the market is mispricing this stock
- [ ] Defined margin of safety
