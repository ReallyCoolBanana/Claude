## 1. GDP: Growth Decelerating

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Q4 2025 GDP (Advance) | **+1.4%** annualized (vs. 2.8% expected, down from 4.4% in Q3) |
| Full Year 2025 GDP | **+2.2%** (vs. 2.8% in 2024) |
| Atlanta Fed GDPNow Q1 2026 | **+2.1%** (down sharply from 3.1% on Feb 20) |
| Consensus 2026 GDP | ~2.4-2.5% (EY, State Street) |

**Analysis:** The economy is clearly decelerating. Q4 2025 came in well below expectations, dragged by a government shutdown that subtracted 0.9pp from growth (government spending contracted 5.1%). Consumer spending slowed to 2.4% with goods purchases actually declining 0.1%. The GDPNow tracker has dropped over a full percentage point in two weeks, signaling further softening as the Iran conflict and tariff uncertainty weigh on activity. The BEA releases the second Q4 estimate on March 13.

## 2. Inflation: Under Control Pre-Shock, Energy Risk Ahead

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| CPI (Feb 2026, y/y) | **2.4%** |
| Core CPI (Feb 2026, y/y) | **2.5%** |
| CPI Monthly (Feb) | +0.3% headline, +0.2% core |
| Food CPI (y/y) | +3.1% |
| PCE (Jan 2026) | ~2.4% (Feb data releases Mar 13) |

**Analysis:** The February CPI print was benign and in-line with expectations. Rent rose only 0.1% (smallest since Jan 2021), a significant positive for the shelter component that has been the stickiest driver. Core inflation at 2.5% is approaching the Fed's 2% target. However, this data is pre-Iran-conflict. WTI crude spiked to $119.48 before settling around $87. If energy prices remain elevated, March and April CPI readings could reaccelerate meaningfully. The "calm before the storm" framing is appropriate -- the inflation picture could deteriorate rapidly depending on the duration of the Iran conflict and Strait of Hormuz disruptions.

## 3. Employment: Deteriorating Rapidly

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Unemployment Rate (Feb 2026) | **4.4%** (up from 4.32% in Jan) |
| Nonfarm Payrolls (Feb) | **-92,000** (vs. +50,000 expected) |
| Average Hourly Earnings (y/y) | **+3.8%** (above +3.7% forecast) |
| Federal Government Job Losses (since Oct 2024) | -330,000 (11% of federal workforce) |
| Prior Revisions | Dec revised to -17K (from +48K); Jan revised to +126K (from +130K) |

**Analysis:** The labor market is flashing warning signs. February marked the third month of job losses in five months. The -92K headline significantly missed expectations. The Kaiser Permanente strike distorted healthcare hiring (-28K), but federal government layoffs (-10K/month and accelerating) are a structural headwind. Critically, wage growth at 3.8% is running above inflation, which supports consumer spending but complicates the Fed's calculus. Prior month revisions were materially negative (-69K combined), suggesting the labor market has been weaker than initially reported.

## 4. Fed Policy: On Hold, Transition Looming

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Fed Funds Rate | **3.50% - 3.75%** |
| Last Action | 3 cuts in 2025 (totaling 75bp) |
| March 17-18 FOMC | Hold expected (~100% probability) |
| 2026 Cut Expectations | 1-2 cuts; market pricing next cut ~September |
| New Fed Chair | Kevin Warsh nominated; Powell's term ends May 2026 |

**Analysis:** The Fed is in a difficult position. The labor market is weakening, which argues for easing, but the Iran conflict has introduced a significant upside risk to inflation via energy prices. The January FOMC vote was 10-2 (Miran and Waller dissented in favor of a cut). The March meeting will include an updated dot plot, which will be closely watched. The impending leadership transition to Kevin Warsh adds policy uncertainty. Markets are pricing 1-2 cuts by year-end, but the war-driven stagflation risk could push any action further out. The Fed is effectively trapped between a weakening economy and potential inflation resurgence.

## 5. Yields: Curve Normal, Recession Probability Rising

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| 10-Year Treasury | **4.16%** |
| 2-Year Treasury | **3.56%** |
| 30-Year Treasury | **4.77%** |
| 10Y-2Y Spread | **+59bp** (positively sloped) |
| 10Y-3M Spread | Oscillating around zero since late Feb |
| NY Fed Recession Probability (Nov 2026) | **25%** |

**Analysis:** The yield curve has normalized after a prolonged inversion (Oct 2022 - Dec 2024). The 10Y-2Y spread is comfortably positive at +59bp. However, the 10Y-3M spread has been flickering between positive and negative since late February, which bears monitoring. The 10Y at 4.16% reflects a market that expects moderate growth, above-target inflation, and limited near-term rate cuts. The 25% recession probability from the NY Fed is elevated but not alarming. Real yields remain positive, which constrains equity valuations, particularly for long-duration growth stocks.

## 7. Geopolitics: Hot War and Trade Disruption

### Iran Conflict (Day 12 -- "Operation Epic Fury")
- US-Israeli strikes began Feb 28; Ayatollah Khamenei killed; son Mojtaba named successor
- Iran retaliating with strikes on US-allied bases across the Gulf
- 8 US service members killed; 1,300+ Iranian civilians reported killed
- Strait of Hormuz traffic near standstill; 20% of global oil/LNG transits this route
- IEA releasing record 400M barrels from strategic reserves
- Iraq shut down 1.5M bbl/day production; Kuwait cutting output
- WTI crude spiked to $119.48, now ~$87 after reserve release plans
- Trump insisting conflict "will be over soon"; Congressional hearings demanded

### Trade / Tariffs
- **Supreme Court struck down IEEPA tariffs** (Feb 20) -- major development
- Blanket 10% tariff on all partners now under Section 122 (150-day limit)
- US-China deal: tariffs cut from 145% to 30% (US) and 125% to 10% (China)
- New Section 301 investigations opened into EU, Mexico, China, and 10+ other partners
- Average household tariff burden: ~$1,500 in 2026

**Analysis:** The geopolitical risk premium is the dominant market driver. The Iran conflict represents the most significant US military engagement since Iraq. Energy supply disruptions are real but partially offset by the IEA reserve release. The Supreme Court's IEEPA ruling was a significant check on tariff escalation, though the administration is pursuing alternative authorities. The US-China tariff reduction is a net positive but could be offset by energy cost inflation. The key variable is conflict duration -- a quick resolution would likely trigger a sharp risk-on rally, while escalation (especially Strait of Hormuz closure) would push oil back toward $100+ and raise recession odds materially.

## Market Environment Summary

The US economy in March 2026 is navigating a **late-cycle environment with acute geopolitical stress**. GDP growth is decelerating (Q4 at 1.4%, GDPNow at 2.1%), the labor market is deteriorating (three months of job losses in five), and inflation -- while currently benign at 2.4% -- faces a significant upside shock from the Iran conflict and $87+ oil. The Fed is on hold at 3.50-3.75% and effectively trapped between a weakening economy and energy-driven inflation risk, with a leadership transition adding further uncertainty. The S&P 500 has given back all YTD gains and broken key technical support, with the VIX elevated at ~25. The defining theme is **rotation**: out of mega-cap tech and into materials, energy, industrials, and small/mid-cap value. This is a **risk-off, late-cycle, stagflation-watch environment**. The dominant risk is conflict escalation pushing oil back above $100, which would tip the balance from "slowdown" to "recession." The dominant opportunity is in real assets and domestic-oriented value plays that benefit from reshoring, commodity inflation, and the narrowing of the growth-value gap. Stock selection should favor companies with pricing power, tangible asset backing, low duration, and limited exposure to global supply chain disruption. Avoid long-duration growth names, rate-sensitive sectors, and companies with significant Middle East or Strait of Hormuz exposure.
